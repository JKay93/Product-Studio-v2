# Labour cost and capability — which problems are worth a bet?

28 October 2026. Online research and PM/Designer discussion; no interviews, implementation, outreach or provider tests.

**Verdict: yes, this is an economically important problem worth investigating. The strongest starting hypothesis is reducing dependence on scarce experienced employees for one repeatable job. A new broad workforce platform is not validated.**

The earlier distribution/construction/F&B shortlist does not constrain this study. User steered the research to labour and capability. Product Manager and Designer independently reconsidered the causes, then discussed possible bets. Orchestrator checked primary evidence and selected the three research tracks below. All proposed segments, product approaches, prices and gates are hypotheses.

## Evidence and its limits

SBF's 2026 manpower study included 511 businesses, 433 SMEs, and broadly reflected SBF membership. In its SME breakdown, 67% cited rising manpower costs and 43% cited insufficient cover for staff undergoing training. This supports the pressure; it does not establish that a particular application would solve it. Informal training is not inherently ineffective. [SBF original study, especially PDF pp.3–5,16 and74](https://www.sbf.org.sg/docs/default-source/about-us/sbf-national-business-survey-2026-manpower-wages-supplement.pdf)

MOM's detailed 2025 vacancy study covers private establishments with at least 25 employees and the public sector. It cannot directly establish prevalence among microbusinesses. Its hard-to-fill non-PMET analysis includes physical demands, working hours and pay. Better learning software will not automatically remedy these conditions. [MRSD, MOM report and methodology](https://stats.mom.gov.sg/iMAS_PdfLibrary/mrsd_JV2025.pdf)

Two named local cases make capability a credible mechanism to investigate:

- **Boon Lay Power Nasi Lemak, August2026:** IAL describes inconsistent preparation/portioning and slow senior-dependent onboarding. The intervention used three SOPs, bilingual visual aids, hands-on practice and internal trainers. Digital tracking was a later possibility. This supports operational learning and coaching, not software causality. [IAL case](https://www.ial.edu.sg/resources/success-stories/boon-lay-power-nasi-lemak-lea2025/)
- **EBA Machinery, July2026:** IAL describes expertise concentrated in experienced staff and improved customer-information gathering before quotation preparation. It reports fewer clarification exchanges and a productivity improvement, without sufficient measurement detail to infer a general effect size. [IAL case](https://www.ial.edu.sg/resources/success-stories/eba-machinery-services-pte-ltd/)

Both are selected programme success stories, not representative samples, independent replications or evidence of willingness to pay us.

## Diagnose before choosing software

| Actual constraint | Possible intervention | Why an app could fail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wage, physical workload, long hours or lack of available workers | Better job conditions, equipment, staffing model, pay or work redesign | A dashboard cannot create willing people or change physical work by itself. |
| Employee lacks a repeatable skill | Demonstration, practice, job aids, feedback and verified readiness | A course/quiz can be completed without being able to do the job. |
| Employee can do it but lacks permission | Clear decision rights and escalation boundaries | More training or AI answers do not confer authority. |
| Expert repeatedly reconstructs missing information | Better intake and handoff before expert review | A form is sufficient if the rules are simple and stable. |
| Available capacity exists but skills/availability are unclear | Qualified cover and targeted cross-training | There may be no genuinely spare, willing, permitted capacity. |
| Work itself is badly designed | Process/layout/tool change | Software may document an inefficient process rather than improve it. |

The outcome to measure is **reliable output per paid hour**, including supervision, rework and software administration. Quality, safe practice and sustainable workload must be held steady or improved. Faster typing, course completion or an AI answer count is not enough.

## Bet 1 — get employees reliably independent on a recurring task

**First priority.** Provisional buyer: operations owner/manager of a 20–100-person service, maintenance or multi-site business, with repeated onboarding or redeployment and an identifiable senior bottleneck. Sector and size remain to be validated.

**Work moment:** a recent starter has completed induction but still pauses, interrupts a senior or repeats a defect on the same task. Trace instruction → demonstration → practice → observed attempt → permitted independent work.

**Possible app:** approved visual/audio job guidance, short contextual practice, supervisor-observed sign-off, and clear boundaries for when to ask or stop. Recurring errors lead to targeted coaching and revised guidance. A qualified human determines readiness; an AI-generated score does not certify physical competence. Company experts own the standard and its updates.

**Why pay:** fewer senior interruptions, less repeat work, quicker reliable independence and more usable capacity from the existing team. Example only: 20 recoverable supervisor hours/month × assumed S$30/hour = S$600/month of time value. It becomes cash saving only if overtime, contractor spend or paid hours actually fall; otherwise state what productive work uses the capacity. A provisional S$100–200/month offer needs value beyond existing tools and sustained content/assessment maintenance.

**Competitive challenge:** eduMe already advertises in-person assessments and point-of-work guidance; Axonify combines coaching/readiness and on-job support. Connecteam's HR & Skills Basic lists US$29/month on annual billing or US$35 monthly for the first30 users, with courses/quizzes. Features and 'course completion is not competence' positioning alone are not differentiation. [eduMe](https://www.edume.com/plans), [Axonify](https://axonify.com/platform/), [Connecteam](https://connecteam.com/pricing/)

**Smallest useful test:** one recurring task, a small group of recent starters and a supervisor. Establish actual interruption/rework and independent-performance criteria; compare a well-designed printed/phone job aid and coaching process with current practice. Test a retained/transfer performance check later, not just immediate compliance. Include the content author's and supervisor's time.

**Reject/narrow if:** most requests are permission-seeking or unusual judgment; equipment/process is the cause; errors are too rare; an existing tool plus better coaching solves it; or maintaining the content/sign-offs adds more work than it removes.

**Potential defensibility:** repeatable expertise for a specific occupation, trusted performance criteria and demonstrably easier deployment. No such advantage is established. A generic document chatbot or LMS is not the proposed investment thesis.

## Bet 2 — make routine intake ready for scarce experts

Provisional buyer: owner/technical sales/service manager of an SME whose senior engineer or specialist repeatedly chases basic details before a quotation or service assessment.

**Work moment:** customer contacts junior staff → incomplete job brief → expert asks for missing details → junior goes back to customer → several clarification rounds → qualified decision. EBA supplies a reported example of this mechanism; it does not prove a software gap.

**Possible app:** role-specific intake that exposes missing or conflicting requirements and teaches why a question matters, assembling a concise evidence-linked brief for the expert. Capture uncertainty explicitly. The expert retains technical suitability, diagnosis, price and scope approval. Start with one bounded service/product family.

**Why pay:** fewer expert minutes and clarification cycles per accepted job, shorter avoidable waiting and additional capacity for valuable work. Test a provisional S$150–300/company/month only against incremental benefit. Faster quotes do not automatically produce more sales or profit if customer demand is unchanged.

**Alternatives:** manufacturer checklists, CRM required fields, a standard form, junior training or administrative support. The incumbent comparison is a well-configured form, not an intentionally poor baseline. Requirements-capture rules can be cheap to implement; the commercial gap must concern consequential failures that persist despite those alternatives.

**Smallest useful test:** replay recent accepted/rejected/incomplete enquiries with junior and expert, then shadow live work manually. Count missing-critical-information rate, expert review/clarification minutes, customer effort and accepted-job quality. Compare with a simple form using the same questions. No automated technical recommendations or external commitments.

**Reject/narrow if:** customers cannot provide the information, site visits are unavoidable, jobs are too bespoke, the senior is an approval bottleneck rather than a knowledge bottleneck, or forms/CRM configuration resolves it. A one-time setup problem may support a service rather than a recurring subscription.

**Potential defensibility:** maintainable occupation-specific intake logic and accepted expert handoffs. Both are unproven and copyable without distribution or demonstrated operational superiority.

## Bet 3 — qualified cover and purposeful cross-training

Provisional buyer: multi-site operations manager with recurring absences, training-release decisions or overtime and some transferable staff.

**Work moment:** someone needs time off or training → manager checks who is competent, available, willing and permitted to cover → overtime/agency staff, reassignment or cancellation.

**Possible app:** verified task capability and restrictions alongside availability to show feasible cover and which specific cross-training would remove a repeated single-person dependency. Qualifications, demonstrated competence and permission must remain separate. Managers approve moves; consider travel, rest, contract restrictions and worker preferences.

**Why pay:** reduced avoidable cover premiums or fewer blocked training opportunities. This only works if some usable capacity exists. Provisional S$100–200/month needs incremental benefit beyond a roster or manager memory.

**Counterevidence:** Connecteam already permits qualification-based shift assignments, including job/shift distinctions. Its scheduling plan is inexpensive; our product must outperform configuration of an existing scheduler. [Connecteam qualification documentation](https://help.connecteam.com/en/articles/8693629-job-schedule-qualifying-users-to-shifts)

**Smallest useful test:** reconstruct the last three cancelled training/expensive-cover incidents. Ask whether a feasible alternative person existed at that time and would have accepted the assignment. Then compare a manually maintained capability/availability sheet with the current roster. Only recurring meaningful exceptions justify a new product.

**Reject/narrow if:** all qualified staff were occupied; no one would accept the shift; moves breach restrictions; the roster already answers it; or capability records cannot stay current at reasonable cost. Showing a skills matrix does not create capacity.

## AI: a possible component, not the proof

A published field study of 5,172 support agents found an average15% increase in issues resolved per hour after AI assistance, with larger gains among less experienced workers. This was one large-company support setting, mainly outside the US; the staggered rollout was not evidence that every SME task or physical skill benefits. [Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, QJE2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae044)

A separate consultant experiment found performance benefits on selected tasks but worse correctness on a deliberately difficult task outside the model's capabilities. The latter involved a particular task and model setting; it is a reason to test limits, not a universal failure rate. [Dell'Acqua et al., published2026](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/dell-acqua-et-al-2026-navigating-the-jagged-technological-frontier_5c589c8c-fbb5-458f-b285-c944746cd717.pdf)

For these bets, AI might help draft reviewed job aids, retrieve approved guidance or spot missing intake information. It must beat the same workflow without AI, accounting for correction, training and administration. No AI or automation is a prerequisite to testing the problem.

## Team judgment and next evidence

PM and Designer agree that task readiness is the first investigation and generic training/hiring/workforce platforms are weak starting points. PM initially considered recurring job redesign ahead of cover commercially; Designer preferred the cheap cover-disproof test and supplied the distinct EBA intake mechanism. Orchestrator selected intake as a separate research track because it has a concrete work moment and measurable expert-capacity outcome; job-redesign software remains a reserve hypothesis. The three are alternatives to investigate, not a roadmap to combine into one platform.

**Buyer judgment:** I would evaluate a narrow trial if my staff show repeated task dependence or expert clarification loss. I would first try coaching, printed/phone aids and existing tools. **Investor judgment:** the problem deserves discovery and a small, measurable validation effort; none of these has proven paid demand, repeatable acquisition or a moat, so a product build remains premature.

The next interviews should ask owners to show the last three times a senior had to intervene, then ask the senior and employee separately what caused it. Distinguish skill, permission, missing information, equipment, allocation and actual capacity. Start with 5–8 similar businesses and one occupation, rather than hundreds of SMEs across unrelated industries. This is a proposed qualitative discovery target, not statistical proof.

Continue only if the same avoidable failure repeats, an existing-tool/process comparison leaves a meaningful gap, workers can use the intervention, someone maintains the rules, and the buyer will fund a bounded pilot. Measure total supervision + execution + rework + system-maintenance time per acceptable output, retained competence and actual use of any released capacity. Subsidy eligibility and enthusiasm are not willingness to pay.

**Recommended starting proposition:** help less-experienced employees perform one defined job correctly and independently, while reducing the senior time needed to support them. Validate the work outcome first; choose whether an app, coaching service, existing product or combination is warranted afterward.
