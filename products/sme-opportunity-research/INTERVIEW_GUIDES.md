# Field interview pack — test problems before pitching products

Use with [the evidence report](REPORT.md). All targets and gates are proposed research methods, not market facts. The user conducts interviews; no outreach has been performed or authorised for the agents.

## Before an interview

Recruit people who recently performed the actual job, including owners who bought competing software or decided not to. Avoid relying only on friends, tech enthusiasts or firms introduced by a single vendor. Do not use grant-supported purchases as proof of voluntary recurring demand. Conduct in the person's preferred language; record translations and uncertainty without assuming an English-only market.

Start with three owners per track, ideally across different sizes/digital setups. Expand only a promising track toward 8–10 qualified firms. Include daily operators and the counterpart whose acceptance matters. These are purposeful qualitative samples; report counts as 'x of y interviewed', never population percentages or statistical validation.

Request a recent ordinary example and a difficult example, preferably within 30–90 days. Ask them to demonstrate in their own systems if comfortable. Offer redaction; do not request passwords, full customer lists, personal IDs or unneeded financial details. Record permission before recording or retaining artifacts. A refusal is a limit on evidence, not evidence that the problem does not exist.

Suggested opening: “I'm researching how businesses handle this work. I'm not selling anything today. Could you walk me through the last real example, including what went well and what was difficult?”

For every track ask:

1. What happened most recently? What triggered it, and when?
2. Show the first record, handoffs and final outcome. Who did what?
3. How often did this happen in the last complete month/cycle? Can we see a count rather than guess?
4. What did it cost in actual time, rework, credits, lost revenue or financing? Which costs were recoverable?
5. Show a successful comparison. What made that case different?
6. What do you use today, including staff, spreadsheets, contractors and software? What works well?
7. What did you last buy or abandon? What was the actual recurring/setup cost, decision and adoption experience?
8. Which existing tool settings or process changes have you tried? Why did they work or fail?
9. Who owns the budget and the relevant data? Who can block a change?
10. If this problem disappeared, what would you actually do differently? What higher-priority problem would still remain?

Do not ask “Would you use an AI app?” or explain our concept before understanding the incident. Do not manufacture a loss by multiplying a recalled annoyance by every employee. Keep observed handling time, estimated time value, cash saving and avoided risk separate.

## A. Distributor order amendments

**Recruit:** locally operating food/grocery distributors with repeat customers, an existing order/accounting system and message/phone ordering. Include at least one incumbent ordering-tool user and one business whose process works well. Speak to owner/operations lead, sales administrator, warehouse/delivery operator and, where possible, a consenting customer.

**Artifacts:** redacted request, confirmation and amendment; order-line export; pick list; delivery acknowledgement; related return/credit. Keep stable order IDs so versions can be reconciled.

**Specific questions**

- Take the last order changed after confirmation. Who learned of the change first? Who learned last?
- Where was the authoritative quantity/pack/date held at each stage? Could someone still see an older version?
- How were short stock, substitutions, cut-off times and cancellations handled?
- Who confirmed the alternative, and how did the warehouse know it was approved?
- Show a duplicate or partial delivery and its invoice/credit. What caused it?
- Which of these did your ERP, supplier portal or ordering provider already catch?
- Why did a missed change happen: missing visibility, a person not following procedure, stale data or no operational capacity?
- How much daily rework remains after normal ordering tools? What would be acceptable data delay?

**Disproof experiment, if later authorised:** reconstruct a representative historical batch containing ordinary, amended, cancelled, duplicate and partially fulfilled orders. Manually build the proposed exception list; compare with the existing process on the same batch. Record missed material changes, false alarms, review time and whether the proposed action was actually possible. Then shadow one working week without writing to ERP or sending messages.

**Kill/narrow:** few consequential amendments; existing software configuration resolves them; no reliable export/access; customers will not acknowledge changes; continuous cleanup or out-of-hours support destroys economics. Do not treat a lack of initial AI capture as proof of a post-confirmation problem.

**Evidence that earns another step:** several independent firms show the same costly failure, ordinary operators can reconcile the needed records, the relevant counterpart accepts the output, and the benefit survives an incumbent comparison.

## B. Specialist subcontractor claims

**Recruit:** one trade initially, such as a specific mechanical/electrical subcontracting discipline, with several concurrent projects and internally prepared claims. Include firms using SitePlus/construction suites or outsourced QS services, not only spreadsheet users. Speak separately to owner, preparing QS/project administrator and a receiving main-contractor QS if permission is available.

**Artifacts:** redacted contract/BOQ, variation instruction, measurement/approval, submitted claim, query/response, certificate and payment dates. Inspect one ordinary accepted case and three delayed/reduced cases. Do not retain confidential whole contracts unless necessary and authorised.

**Specific questions**

- Walk through the last reduced certificate. Which line changed, and what reason was given?
- Was the instruction authorised? Where was approval or rejection recorded?
- Which support was missing at submission? Did supplying it change the result?
- Was the dispute quantity, rate, scope, quality, timing, payer cash or something else?
- What had already been claimed/certified? How did you avoid double counting and track retention?
- Whose submission format was mandatory? Would an evidence index/export be accepted without another portal?
- What does the QS reconstruct each month? Why do existing tools or templates fail?
- Show a case with complete accepted evidence but late payment. What could software have changed there?

**Disproof experiment, if later authorised:** a qualified commercial reviewer reconstructs a completed cycle with approved/unapproved variations, revised quantities, prior certificates and retention. Produce a claim-line missing-support list in the normal recipient format. Compare preparation time, errors and receiver queries against current practice. No automatic legal advice, deadlines, certification or serving claims.

**Kill/narrow:** complete evidence would not have changed certification; delays mainly reflect liquidity or bargaining power; current tools suffice; receiving QS rejects the format; every packet still requires bespoke expert reconstruction. A large overdue balance is not automatically recoverable product value.

**Evidence that earns another step:** a repeated documentary failure in one trade, correctable from available records, acknowledged by the receiving QS and expensive enough to fund the solution.

## C. F&B input-cost decisions

**Recruit:** independent 2–10-outlet owners, including groups already using Foodrazor/Nomni or a cost consultant, and a well-run comparison business. Speak to owner, chef/procurement lead, accounts and outlet managers. Avoid conflating small hawkers with chains.

**Artifacts:** three recent material ingredient price/specification changes; before/after quotes/invoices/credits; pack sizes, approved recipes and relevant yields; decision/change instructions; subsequent accepted deliveries, stock and, if needed, dish sales. Include one change where the owner deliberately did nothing.

**Specific questions**

- When did you first notice the change? Was the information late or simply difficult to act on?
- Which alternatives did you actually consider? Why were some rejected?
- How did quality, yield, MOQ, delivery charges, credit terms and customer expectations affect the choice?
- Who could negotiate, substitute, alter a portion or change menu price?
- Which recipe, purchasing and outlet instructions had to change? Where did execution diverge?
- What did your current app or chef review already show? Why was that insufficient?
- What evidence told you the decision helped? Could lower purchasing simply be stock depletion or lower sales?
- How much effort is spent maintaining ingredient matches, recipes and counts? Would more logging make the job worse?

**Disproof experiment, if later authorised:** one outlet, roughly five high-spend ingredients and a small number of dishes over one replenishment cycle. Verify pack units, tax treatment, returns, accepted quantities and relevant usable yields manually. Compare an evidence-linked decision sheet with the existing process/app using the same review time. The owner performs any supplier contact or price decision. Count realised credits/quality-accepted savings net of freight, spoilage and extra effort; separate labour time. Do not claim full realised margin without sufficient stock and sales data.

**Kill/narrow:** owner already knew promptly; no feasible better action existed; existing tools/support cover the step; culinary/recipe maintenance becomes ongoing consultancy; no credible way to attribute a benefit. A price-change alert alone is not a product gap.

**Evidence that earns another step:** repeated avoidable decisions or execution failures, actionable alternatives, sustainable records and measured incremental benefit after incumbent comparison.

## Decision and stop rules

After the first nine owner conversations, compare evidence rather than enthusiasm. A suggested expansion rule is at least two artifact-backed incidents in the same narrow track plus a plausible reason current alternatives fail. Do not force all tracks into pilots.

For a track expanded to 8–10 firms, our proposed bar before recommending a build is:

- At least five independent firms demonstrate the same recent, consequential failure, with named budget owners. This is a business gate, not statistical proof.
- Several can lawfully supply representative records and the output is usable by daily operators/receiving counterparts.
- A matched replay beats the current process and a sensible incumbent configuration on net effort or recoverable money, with no material accuracy regression.
- Incremental benefit plausibly exceeds 3× the proposed fee; customer acquisition, setup and ongoing support can be measured rather than assumed free.
- In a separately authorised commercial-validation stage, seek at least three genuinely paid design partners in the same segment and continued use over two natural operating cycles. A free trial, friendly promise or grant application does not meet this bar.

Stop or narrow if counterexamples show the actual bottleneck is different, incumbent adoption is the sensible answer, or benefits cannot be measured. Do not keep adding features to explain away negative interviews.

## Copyable interview record

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Firm code / industry / size / outlet or project count | Anonymised unless publication is authorised |
| Participant role / budget authority / recruitment source | Distinguish owner, user and vendor |
| Date / language / recording and artifact permission | State what may be retained |
| Existing tools / actual recurring and setup spend | Confirm currency, billing and subsidies |
| Last incident / successful comparison / dates | Observed, reported or estimated? |
| Trigger → handoffs → authoritative record → outcome | Link only permitted redacted evidence |
| Root cause / alternative explanations | Include liquidity, quality, staffing and power |
| Frequency / time / cash loss / recoverable share | Keep different value measures separate |
| Existing-tool fix attempted / result | Configure-before-build challenge |
| Feasible different action / who must accept it | Do not substitute a hypothetical benefit |
| Contradictory evidence / unanswered questions | Record reasons against the idea |
| Decision | Reject, narrow, investigate or later-authorised pilot |

Report verbatim statements only with permission and attribution rules. Keep raw notes separate from interpretation. No conclusion about population demand follows merely from a small convenience sample.
