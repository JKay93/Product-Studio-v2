## Findings — 3 November 2026

No approach is production-ready for our requirements on the evidence reviewed. The three families below can operate without a competitor's private generation API; that does not establish ownership of their underlying intellectual property or eliminate licence/runtime dependencies. User clarifies: existing pretrained LoRAs may be considered; own training remains deferred. This is research scope, not authority to download weights or run inference.

| Family | Actual control and available artifacts | Feasibility verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pixelated Image Abstraction (PIA)** | Published joint optimization of spatial feature mapping and palette; input image to explicit W×H output. A modern Rust implementation is MIT; no learned weights or generation service. | Conditional research baseline only. No semantic understanding of eyes versus outlines. Inspected implementation drops alpha, needs build/runtime work and has source-level fidelity concerns. Not a ready replacement for our converter. |
| **SD-πXL** | Frozen diffusion guidance optimizes a target H×W palette-index representation. Text, palette and optional source/edge/depth conditioning. Public MIT implementation and downloadable component models. | Strongest explicit grid/palette mechanism in this shortlist; defer execution for current interactive/cost goals. Published runtime and recommended memory are substantial; no verified 8 GB configuration. |
| **Pretrained pixel adapter on FLUX.2 Klein 4B** | Existing Limbicnation pixel-art LoRA plus independently runnable base/pipeline, with public inference weights. Base supports reference inputs. | Sole conditional candidate for independently operated model generation; no native 16×32/32×64 guarantee, confirmed laptop fit, artistic pass or verified training-data provenance. Not selected for production or execution. |

**PIA evidence and limits.** The [2012 author page](https://cragl.cs.gmu.edu/pixelate/) describes joint spatial/palette optimization; the [2013 extension](https://cragl.cs.gmu.edu/pixelate/journal.html) adds manual constraints. Those manual controls must not be attributed to every implementation. The [Rust implementation](https://github.com/AlexandreBinninger/pixelization) exposes explicit width/height via library and CLI, with an [MIT licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexandreBinninger/pixelization/main/LICENSE). Its [manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexandreBinninger/pixelization/main/Cargo.toml) requires Rust and system OpenBLAS; Windows build is unverified. Cargo/rustc are on this machine's PATH; BLAS and build success were not established.

Static inspection of [pia.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexandreBinninger/pixelization/main/src/pixelizer/pia.rs) finds RGB input/output with opaque alpha, all-pairs source-pixel/superpixel assignment, cost called with pixel-count arguments 1,1 (cancelling its intended ratio), and smoothing using current-position offsets rather than neighboring superpixel positions. These are implementation fidelity/performance concerns, not reproduced failures or proof that the publication fails. No benchmark or build was run. Do not port its code unquestioningly or promise it will solve facial/outline semantics. An [older author-derived C++ fork](https://github.com/fHachenberg/pix) is labelled GPL-3.0 with OpenCV dependencies; it is not an MIT substitute.

**SD-πXL evidence and limits.** The [paper and supplement](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06236) report 6,000–10,000 optimization steps taking roughly 1.5–2.5 hours on RTX 4090, mostly at 64×64. Those are author results, not our measurements or a latency guarantee at 16×32. The [official README](https://github.com/AlexandreBinninger/SD-piXL) recommends 24 GB VRAM and offers smaller-model/ControlNet suggestions without validating our 8 GB laptop. Training-free means frozen foundation weights, with per-image optimization still required. Code is [MIT](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexandreBinninger/SD-piXL/main/LICENSE); [component identifiers](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexandreBinninger/SD-piXL/main/pipelines/distillation/__init__.py) include SDXL/SSD1B, TAESDXL and ControlNets, whose separate licences must be retained (SDXL is [Open RAIL++-M](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0/raw/main/LICENSE.md), not MIT).

The [entry point](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexandreBinninger/SD-piXL/main/main.py) accepts height,width: width16/height32 is --size 32,16. [PixelArt renderer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexandreBinninger/SD-piXL/main/models/pixelart.py) has H×W×palette-count parameters and discrete argmax output. [Pipeline source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AlexandreBinninger/SD-piXL/main/pipelines/SD_piXL.py) converts input to RGB, preprocesses square conditioning and can save rectangular grids stretched to a square preview. It therefore needs explicit aspect-preserving conditioning, native export and alpha treatment before a sprite comparison. A larger PNG is not itself a grid failure. No claim of fidelity, transparency or animation consistency.

**Pretrained model evidence and limits.** [Limbicnation card](https://huggingface.co/Limbicnation/pixel-art-lora) specifies Klein4B, 512×512 delivery, four steps and guidance1. It describes style, not a target logical-cell guarantee; “16-bit” is not sixteen pixels. [Creator files](https://huggingface.co/Limbicnation/pixel-art-lora/tree/main) include a 325 MB Diffusers-native adapter republished at 0ac8e5c, separately from a ComfyUI variant. Future loading must specify the correct weight file. Base/adapter and Diffusers licence labels are Apache2.0; the card's CC0/1,010-image claims conflict with its [linked 500-row synthetic Apache-labelled dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Limbicnation/pixel-art-character). Licence labels alone do not verify provenance or commercial safety.

The [base model card](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B) advertises approximately 13 GB and documents CPU offload; the [BFL repository](https://github.com/black-forest-labs/flux2) advertises approximately 8 GB. Neither establishes the complete adapter pipeline on our measured RTX 4060 Laptop GPU/8188 MiB. Peak VRAM/system RAM, exact package compatibility, offload latency and alpha remain unmeasured. No numeric inference-cost quote is justified; no rental was priced or authorized. Prior standard FLUX tests never loaded this adapter and do not settle its quality.

Within this family, Krea pixel adapter was screened but not advanced: [creator](https://huggingface.co/e-n-v-y/Krea-2-Pixel-Art) offers 32/64/128 square variants, but rectangular identity support is unverified, the base is gated and has its own [Community Licence](https://www.krea.ai/krea-2-licensing), and the recommended refiner lacks a clear top-level licence. It still uses grid collapse/palette reduction; that is not evidence of semantic redesign. This is an excluded variant within the pretrained-model family, not a fourth approach.
